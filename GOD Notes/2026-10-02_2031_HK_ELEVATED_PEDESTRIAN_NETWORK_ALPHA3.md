## Field observation

Hong Kong's second-level pedestrian infrastructure is not decorative urban design; it functions as a real routing layer.

Observed reusable patterns:
- Admiralty → Central can be traversed through elevated walkways, podiums, malls and connected buildings rather than relying only on ground-level streets.
- Wan Chai → Exhibition Centre has a strong elevated connection logic: the pedestrian layer can connect rail / commercial / office nodes while avoiding repeated descent to street level.
- Underground space often carries MTR + dense commercial functions.
- Ground level carries roads, vehicle flow, entrances and local street life.
- The elevated layer carries fast pedestrian routing, mall/building interfaces, weather protection, pause points and cross-block connectivity.

This produces a genuinely **3D city graph** rather than a flat map:

1. Underground — rail + commercial + transfer
2. Ground — street + vehicle + local frontage
3. Elevated — footbridge + podium + mall + office interfaces
