# Chater House Field Lab — PACT × PBCT × Gen3 Interview Mode

**Timestamp:** 2026-10-07 18:04 HKT  
**Location:** Chater House, Central, Hong Kong  
**Mode:** Alpha Time / field observation

## Core insight

**PBCT is a large-scale technical PACT.**

PACT is the ability to coordinate people, organizations, resources, incentives, capital, and decisions. PBCT applies the same closure logic to technical systems: collaborate with researchers, software/data/infra engineers, execution, risk, operations, and other technical stakeholders; define interfaces; ask for compute or funding when needed; and turn a strategy into a production system that actually works.

The strongest PBCT operator is therefore not only a coder. They can:
- understand the technical system,
- communicate across specialist teams,
- ask the right people for resources,
- coordinate dependencies,
- close research → production,
- and keep the system operable after launch.

## Chater House as a field lab

Treat Chater House as a **PACT × PBCT observation node** rather than only a landmark.

Useful things to study here:
- how different firms structure people and teams,
- how employees move and socialize around the office,
- how hiring/recruiting interfaces with candidates,
- differences in visible workplace norms,
- what technical and operational roles appear to exist,
- how a global trading firm may connect research, engineering, operations, and recruiting.

### Observation discipline

Evening office egress, clothing, group behavior, and public-space interactions are useful **signals**, not proof of identity or role.

Do not infer that a passerby definitely works for Gen3, is HR/operations/software engineering, or comes from a particular country based only on appearance. Record these as unverified field impressions until confirmed through conversation or public information.

One tentative cultural observation from the field: some groups around the target trading-firm environment appeared more casually dressed than groups the user has associated with large investment-bank environments such as JPMorgan. Treat this as a hypothesis about workplace culture, not a conclusion from one observation.

## Gen3 objective: one role at hiring-bar level

The practical target is not "apply everywhere." It is:

> **Reach the standard of at least one clearly defined Gen3 role, then prepare deeply enough to survive the full process.**

Daily preparation should make the role increasingly concrete:
- role benchmark,
- technical bar,
- probability/math/coding/system-design bar where relevant,
- communication and behavioral bar,
- firm/product understanding,
- mock interview repetition,
- public artifacts and evidence,
- targeted recruiter / employee outreach.

The selection process may be long and globally competitive. That is not a reason to treat it as mysterious. Convert it into a sequence of measurable gates.

## Recruiter leverage — correctly framed

Recruiters and former campus recruiters can influence process flow by:
- identifying role fit,
- routing a profile,
- clarifying hiring needs,
- helping a candidate enter or progress through the process,
- providing context on timing and team needs.

But recruiter leverage does **not** replace the hiring bar.

The candidate-side job is therefore:
1. become obviously compatible with at least one role;
2. make the evidence legible;
3. enter the correct process;
4. perform repeatedly under interview conditions.

## Daily "Gen3 interview mode"

Operate as if there is an interview every day.

Each day should improve at least one of:
- technical depth,
- system design / PBCT,
- probability / math / coding,
- market intuition,
- English communication,
- concise self-introduction,
- role-specific stories,
- recruiter / employee graph,
- mock interview execution.

The goal is compounding readiness, not emotional waiting.

## Connection protocol around the office

Public-space outreach should stay low-pressure and respectful.

A good sequence is:
1. identify whether someone is open to talking;
2. confirm their firm/team rather than assuming;
3. ask one specific question about role/team/culture;
4. briefly explain relevant background;
5. request LinkedIn only if the conversation is naturally positive.

Do not obstruct people leaving work or turn the office entrance into repeated unsolicited pursuit. The highest-value connection is voluntary, specific, and easy to continue online.

## PACT × PBCT convergence

The long-run picture:

`people graph + organizational understanding + funding/resources + technical collaborators + research + engineering + live system + feedback`

PACT gives access, coordination, and organizational closure.  
PBCT gives technical depth and production closure.  
Together they make it possible to move from an idea to a real institutional system.
