# Direction × Autonomy × Funding — Earned and Negotiated

Date: 2026-10-03
Status: GOD Note / lifelong operating principle

## Core doctrine

Direction, Autonomy and Funding are rarely fully guaranteed by a job title, team name or PhD label.

A position creates a structural opportunity set and ceiling. The realized amount of D/A/F is often produced through:

**capability proof × trust × communication × bargaining × organizational context.**

## PACT role

PACT is a bottom-layer execution skill for obtaining workable conditions:
- communication,
- expectation setting,
- bargaining,
- scope negotiation,
- stakeholder alignment,
- resource requests,
- boundary setting,
- making the case for greater ownership.

The banking year is therefore not wasted even if banking is not the long-term destination: communication and bargaining are reusable primitives.

## PBCT role

PBCT is what makes autonomy sustainable.

Autonomy is easier to obtain when there is evidence that one can:
- independently solve problems,
- build systems,
- research,
- debug,
- deliver outputs,
- make good technical judgments,
- finish work without excessive supervision.

The stronger the delivery evidence, the easier it is for a manager / PI to delegate.

## PCCT role

PCCT helps identify:
- what the institution actually values,
- where the real decision rights sit,
- which constraints are structural,
- how to frame a proposal so it fits the organization,
- when to negotiate and when a role has a hard ceiling.

## Job / PhD symmetry

The same logic applies to both work and PhD relationships.

A manager or PI may grant more ownership, direction-setting freedom and resources once confidence exists that the person can use them well.

Therefore the target is not merely to "find a job with D/A/F". It is to:
1. choose an environment with enough structural room,
2. demonstrate capability,
3. build trust,
4. communicate and bargain for scope/resources,
5. execute strongly enough to preserve and expand that autonomy.

## Lifelong rule

PACT × PBCT × PCCT are lifelong compound skills.

Keep improving:
- ability to win room,
- ability to use room,
- ability to understand the system in which that room exists.
